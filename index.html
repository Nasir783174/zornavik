Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a cordless stick vacuum and a robot vacuum?
A cordless stick vacuum is a lightweight, battery-powered vacuum you push by hand — great for quick, controlled cleanups. A robot vacuum cleans on its own schedule without you holding it, which is convenient for maintenance cleaning but generally provides less deep-cleaning suction than a good stick or upright, especially on thick carpet.
How much should I spend on a good vacuum cleaner in 2026?
Most shoppers get excellent results in the $60–$130 range for a corded upright or cordless stick. Robot vacuum and mop combos with self-emptying docks typically run $400–$1,200 depending on features like object recognition and hot-water mop washing.
Are robot vacuums actually good for pet hair?
Modern robot vacuums with high-kPa suction and tangle-resistant rollers handle everyday shedding well, but for homes with heavy-shedding pets or thick carpet, pairing a robot vacuum with an occasional deep clean from an upright or cordless stick still gives the best results.
Do I need a HEPA filter if no one in my home has allergies?
A HEPA filter isn't mandatory, but it does trap far more fine dust and allergens than a basic foam filter, which improves indoor air quality for everyone — not just allergy sufferers. It's a worthwhile upgrade if the price difference is small.
How long should a good cordless vacuum's battery last?
Look for at least 40–50 minutes of runtime on a mid or eco power setting. Nearly every cordless model loses runtime fast on its highest suction setting — some drop to 12–20 minutes — so check both numbers, not just the headline runtime.
Bagged vs. bagless vacuums — which is better?
Bagless vacuums are cheaper to run long-term and let you see when the bin is full, but emptying them can kick up dust. Bagged vacuums cost a little more in replacement bags but seal in allergens better when emptying. Most vacuums we review today are bagless.
